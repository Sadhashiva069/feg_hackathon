FEG Hackathon 2026 · Challenge 3 · before / after measurement · 8 Sep 2026

Same certified bundle, byte for byte. Same Cloudflare tunnel, same Chrome, same link profiles and the same engine milestones as the baseline report. What changed is everything around the bundle: a lobby that pre-loads and pre-boots the game the player is most likely to open next, a service worker that owns the cache, an edge that serves compressed immutable files, and a prediction model fitted on the sample event log.

1. Before and after, configuration by configuration

Every row is one of the baseline report's configurations, re-run through the tunnel with the accelerated lobby. Before is the time from opening the game URL to the Play button (engine event PRIMARY_ASSETS_LOADED). After is the time from the tap on the lobby tile (pointerdown) to the game frame being on screen with that same Play button already on it; the game was pre-loaded and pre-booted while the player was in the lobby. The axis is logarithmic because the two sides differ by three orders of magnitude.

2. What a tap can find, on the emulated 4G phone

The sub-500 ms number depends on what state the predicted game is in when the tile is tapped. All six states were measured on the same emulated phone, same link, same run day. Only the two darker bars are the accelerated steady state; the others are what a prediction miss, a low-memory device or a first visit look like.

3. p50 and p95 of the predicted path

Twenty independent cold-browser sessions on the emulated 4G phone, each: open the lobby, consent, wait for the predicted game to be parked, tap. This is the distribution behind the M1/M2 numbers for a correctly predicted launch.

4. How often the prediction is right

The model was fitted and replayed on the sample event log (13,682 launches, 65 players, one month). It is causal: every prediction uses only launches that happened before it. Weights were chosen on the first 60 % of the month and the numbers below are from the last 40 %.

What that means for the whole population of launches

Combining the replay with the measured times per state gives a projected tap-to-Play-button distribution over all holdout launches. The gap between consecutive launches in the log decides whether the pre-load had time to finish; the worker keeps a player's last three games on disk, so a relaunch of a recent game is never worse than the prefetched state.

5. What the player sees

Frames captured by the harness on the emulated 4G phone: the lobby with the predicted game parked behind it, the moment of the tap, and the game with its Play button. There is no blank screen; the poster is on screen within the next frame after the tap on every path.

7. What was changed, and what was not

    Guardrails. The bundle directory is served byte-for-byte under a versioned path; nothing in it was edited. The responsible-gambling gate (self-exclusion register, age verification, reality check) runs before the reveal on every path, including the pre-booted one; the game's own splash and Play button are never auto-clicked. Pre-loading is opt-in per player, never on data saver or 2G, and limited to one pre-booted game with a 10-minute idle limit. Player identifiers stay synthetic in the sandbox; the prediction model lives outside the repository.

    8. Method and reproduction

    Path of every byte

    The edge server (src/edge/server.js) serves the lobby and the untouched bundle from one origin, published through a cloudflared quick tunnel. Chrome 152 is driven over the DevTools protocol with a fresh browser context per run (no HTTP cache, no storage). Link profiles are applied at the origin, per client, because Chrome's own network emulation does not throttle service-worker fetches; the profiles reproduce the baseline's cold numbers.

    Clock

    All marks are epoch milliseconds, so the lobby and the game frame share one clock. t0 is the pointerdown on the tile. Engine milestones come from the game's own event emitter, hooked from the same-origin lobby before the engine boots.
